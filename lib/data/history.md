# Tarot and Craft history notes (original summaries, for site "About" copy)
- Tarot began in 15th-century northern Italy as a card game (*trionfi*, later *tarocchi*). The earliest surviving decks are hand-painted luxury decks such as the Visconti-Sforza (c. mid-1400s). Card divination with tarot is documented from the 18th century onward.
- Court de Gébelin (1781) and later French occultists (Etteilla, Lévi, Papus) claimed ancient Egyptian, Kabbalistic or "Bohemian" (Romani) origins. Historians led by Michael Dummett have shown that these claims have no basis. **Site copy should not claim Egyptian or ancient origins.** Present them as part of tarot's legend.
- The Golden Dawn (founded in 1888) systematized the links between tarot, astrology and Hebrew letters (Book T). Waite and Pamela Colman Smith's deck (1909) made illustrated minor arcana the modern norm.
- Different occult systems map the Hebrew letters differently. Papus and Lévi assign them differently from the Golden Dawn, and Crowley swaps Heh and Tzaddi. This site uses Book T.
- Modern Paganism and Wicca are 20th-century revivals. Ronald Hutton's *The Triumph of the Moon* (1999) is the standard academic history. It shows these traditions draw on folk magic, ceremonial magic and Romanticism rather than an unbroken ancient line. British "cunning folk" were real historical folk-magic practitioners who offered protection charms and healing.
- Astrology: the planetary rulers of the signs follow Ptolemy's *Tetrabiblos* (2nd c.). William Lilly's *Christian Astrology* (1647) preserves the traditional framework. The modern outer-planet co-rulers (Uranus, Neptune, Pluto) are 19th- and 20th-century additions.
